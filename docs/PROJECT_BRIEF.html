The problem

At 28,000 feet, a healthy engine looks sick

A conventional engine instrument system watches each channel against a fixed redline. On a light aircraft at 8,000 ft that works. On a MALE UAV climbing to 30,000 ft on a twenty-hour sortie it fails in both directions at once.

Climb high enough and a healthy engine starts tripping alarms. Manifold pressure falls as the turbo runs out of air, rated power derates from 200 HP to 110 HP, exhaust temperatures drift. Nothing is broken — the atmosphere changed. The operator learns to ignore the alarm.

Meanwhile a genuinely failing engine sits comfortably inside every redline. An injector clogging on cylinder 3 pushes EGT₃ up by 40 °C, well under the 880 °C continuous limit, so nothing trips — right up until it does.

The trap is that a raw sensor reading carries two things at once: the state of the engine, and the state of the flight. You cannot threshold your way out of that, and a black-box model trained on raw telemetry inherits the same problem — it just hides it behind a confidence score.

The idea

Diagnose the gap from physics, never the reading

A Mean Value Engine Model runs in lockstep with the engine, fed the same altitude, ambient conditions and throttle. It predicts what a healthy engine would be doing right now. Diagnosis then happens on the difference, normalised per channel:

rᵢ  =  ( ymeasured − yphysics )  /  σchannel

Fifteen channels — RPM, manifold pressure, fuel flow, oil pressure and temperature, four CHT, four EGT, vibration RMS, bus voltage — reduced to one vector of standard deviations from thermodynamic expectation.

Because the physics baseline has already absorbed altitude, throttle and ambient temperature, a healthy engine sits at r ≈ 0 everywhere in the envelope — sea level or 30,000 ft, idle or full power. The learning problem stops being “recognise this engine” and becomes “recognise a departure from physics,” which is a far smaller and far more transferable problem. Every model downstream sees residuals. None of them ever sees a raw sensor value.

Read the EIS column carefully. A perfect 0.00 % false-alarm rate alongside a macro F1 of 0.25 is not a strength — the thresholds are set so wide that almost nothing real trips them. The black-box model has the opposite problem: strong in-distribution accuracy, and an 11.74 % false-alarm rate because high-altitude telemetry looks anomalous to a model with no physics baseline for derating.

The ablation isolates that effect directly, training the same network on raw channels instead of residuals. Physics anchoring is worth +0.0597 macro F1 and 14.31 points of false-alarm rate. Restricted to healthy frames above 20,000 ft, where derating is largest, the gap widens to 0.70 % against 18.42 %. That is the whole argument for the residual representation, measured rather than asserted.

Unsupervised residual autoencoder trained only on healthy flight, with a threshold calibrated at μ + 3σ — so it flags failure modes absent from the taxonomy entirely.

The case that decides sorties

A dead probe is not a dead cylinder

A thermocouple fails open on cylinder 3. EGT₃ falls toward ambient. To a redline system this is an engine in the process of dying, and a perfectly serviceable aircraft gets recalled.

EngineTwin checks EGT₃ against CHT₃ and fuel flow, finds the neighbouring channels entirely nominal, and reports sensor probe defect — engine healthy. Overall health barely moves. The mission continues.

Boundaries

What this is not

A reviewer who finds an unstated limitation discounts everything else in the submission. So, stated plainly:

  • Everything is simulated. The engine, the instrumentation and the faults are models. No hardware-in-the-loop validation has been performed, and nothing here is certified for airworthiness.
  • The estimator is not an EKF. It is a linear Kalman filter with a complementary prediction step toward the physics model. There is no non-linear transition function and no Jacobian, so the covariance is a tuned noise-rejection parameter, not a calibrated uncertainty.
  • The attribution is not SHAP. It is gradient × input — a first-order local ranking chosen for the 50 ms edge budget, without Shapley’s local-accuracy or consistency guarantees.
  • The RUL interval is a trend interval, constructed from the spread of the degradation fit rather than from a distribution-free coverage guarantee. Conformal prediction is the honest upgrade.
  • Per-frame inference degrades under extreme noise. At five times instrumentation sigma the classifier names a false mechanical fault on roughly one frame in six. The annunciator votes over a rolling window, so it does not reach the operator — but it is a real weakness.
The ground station

An engine you can point at

The operator console runs at 20 Hz in any browser, with no internet route required — every asset is served from the twin’s own host. The engine is rendered as a live model rather than a diagram: each cylinder is shaded by its thermal deviation from the model, in sigma, so a cold-day 700 °C and a hot-day 760 °C both read as nominal, and only a genuine departure changes colour.

Direct selection

Every part is pickable. Hover to identify it, click to inspect it — and the inspector answers the question the model alone cannot: the measured value, what physics expected, and the residual between them in standard deviations. Cylinders, EGT probes, turbocharger, oil sump, crankcase and propeller are each their own component, because a dead thermocouple and a dead cylinder are different findings.

One annunciator scale

The colours on this page are the console’s colours. Instrument blue means one thing everywhere — what physics says — carrying the model-expected traces, the residual column and the 3D selection outline. Amber and red are spent only on genuine exceedance, and sensor traces stay greyscale so hue is never wasted on identity.

Five minutes at the console

The demonstration arc, in the order it makes the argument.

01

Watch it loiter

High-altitude cruise. Every strip shows the measurement tracking the physics baseline inside its ±3σ band. The absolute numbers look nothing like sea level, and nothing complains.

02

Break the oil system

Under three seconds later the trigger fires, the classifier names OIL_PRESSURE_LOSS, the sump goes red on the model and the directive reads return to base.

03

Break a sensor instead

EGT₃ falls toward ambient. This is the case a redline system aborts a healthy aircraft over. Health barely moves; the mission continues.

04

Ask it why

Open the evidence drawer, or click the implicated part in the model. The diagnosis arrives with its ranked residual channels and an inspection window in flight hours.

05

Land and debrief

Pick the sortie from the recorder, read its post-flight report, then replay the whole flight through the same telemetry path — the console replays without knowing the difference.

Verification

Every number regenerates on your machine

Nine suites — physics, AI layer, end-to-end fault propagation, leakage audit, adversarial stress, baseline comparison, architectural ablation, multi-engine transfer, edge profiling. Every suite exits non-zero on failure, so a green run means all nine genuinely passed. No internet connection is required at any point.
