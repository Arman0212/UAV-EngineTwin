The problem

Why threshold alarms fail at 28,000 ft

A conventional engine instrument system watches each channel against a fixed redline. On a light aircraft at 8,000 ft, that works. On a MALE UAV climbing from sea level to 30,000 ft on a 20-hour sortie, it fails in both directions at once.

⚠

Climb high enough and a healthy engine looks sick. Power derates from 200 HP to 110 HP, EGT drifts, MAP falls — nothing is broken, but every fixed threshold reads it as a fault. The operator learns to ignore alarms.

The trap

A raw sensor reading carries two things at once

Engine state

Is the injector clogging? Is the turbo losing boost? Is a bearing wearing?

+
Flight state

What altitude? What ambient pressure? What temperature? What throttle setting?

You cannot threshold your way out of that. A black-box model trained on raw telemetry inherits the same problem — it just hides it behind a confidence score.

The idea

Diagnose the gap from physics, never the reading

rᵢ  =  ( ymeasured − yphysics )  /  σchannel

A Mean Value Engine Model runs in lockstep with the engine, fed the same altitude, ambient conditions and throttle. Because the baseline has already absorbed altitude and derating, a healthy engine sits at r ≈ 0 across the whole envelope — sea level or 30,000 ft. Every model downstream sees residuals. None of them ever sees a raw sensor value.

The representation

The engine exists at five levels at once

A digital twin that only mirrors the sensor stream cannot tell you what the sensor got wrong. This one keeps every layer separately, and the gaps between them are where the diagnosis lives.

01 Physical truth The engine as it actually is, faults included. Nothing downstream is allowed to see this.
02 Raw sensor Truth seen through per-channel noise, first-order lag, ADC quantisation and sample rate.
03 Filtered estimate Twelve states fused from measurement and physics; coasts on physics when the datalink drops.
04 Healthy baseline A second engine model, fed the same flight, that never sees the fault. This is the reference.
05 AI health layer Anomaly score, fault class, severity, remaining life and ranked attribution.
The physics

A Mean Value Engine Model, not a curve fit

The baseline is an integrator, not a lookup. Every tick it advances manifold filling dynamics, turbocharger boost against the altitude derating law, the crankshaft torque balance, four independent cylinder thermal states and the oil circuit. That is what lets it hold r ≈ 0 through a climb, a throttle change and a cold day alike.

◇

Manifold & boost

Filling dynamics with a 0.35 s time constant; compressor output derates above the ~10,000 ft critical altitude as backpressure rises.

◇

Torque balance

Indicated torque against friction and propeller load, integrated on crank inertia — so RPM is a consequence, never a setpoint.

◇

Four thermal states

Each cylinder carries its own head and exhaust temperature, so a single-cylinder fault has somewhere to show up.

◇

Calibrated envelope

200 HP at sea level and 10,000 ft, 150 HP at 20,000 ft, 110 HP at 30,000 ft — the published VRDE derating curve.

The instruments

Residuals are measured in sigma, so sigma has to be real

Every channel is normalised by its own noise figure. Those figures come from the instrument model rather than from tuning: a thermocouple is slow and coarse, a magnetic pickup is fast and clean, and the residual vector has to respect the difference or a quiet channel drowns a loud one.

The fault library

Ten classes, each injectable at any severity and ramp

Faults are parametric perturbations of the physics or the instrumentation, not label flips on a dataset. Each one has a mechanism and a signature the residual vector can actually carry.

HEALTHY no perturbation r ≈ 0 across all fifteen channels
LEAN_MIXTURE_CYL3 injector partially clogged EGT₃ climbs, thermal gradient across the bank tilts
RICH_MIXTURE_CYL1 injector leaking EGT₁ falls while total fuel flow rises
COOLING_DEGRADATION_CYL2 baffle or airflow blockage CHT₂ rises fast; EGT largely unaffected
OIL_PRESSURE_LOSS relief valve leak oil pressure below 2.0 bar, temperature trailing
TURBO_BOOST_DEFICIENCY wastegate stuck, compressor fouling MAP deficit that deepens with altitude
CYLINDER_MISFIRE_TIMING timing jitter, valve seating RPM instability and a 0.5× vibration order

The case that decides sorties

A dead probe is not a dead cylinder

Fixed-threshold EIS

EGT₃ falls toward ambient. Every redline for that channel is breached at once. The system reports an engine in the process of dying, and a perfectly serviceable aircraft is recalled.

vs
EngineTwin

EGT₃ is checked against CHT₃ and fuel flow. The neighbouring channels are entirely nominal, so no combustion event explains the reading. Verdict: sensor probe defect, engine healthy.

This is why the probe defect is a first-class member of the taxonomy rather than a heuristic bolted on afterwards. It is the single failure mode most likely to abort a healthy sortie — and on the held-out set it is separated at 1.000 precision and 1.000 recall across 2,768 frames.

Data integrity

Seventy-eight independent missions, split by sortie

Splitting by row would put adjacent 50 ms samples on both sides of the boundary and turn the test score into fiction. Splits are partitioned by whole flight, and each sortie draws its own mission shape — ceiling, loiter band, throttle and phase fractions — on top of an ISA offset.

SplitSortiesFramesPurpose
Healthy training1018,000autoencoder and σ calibration
Fault training3664,800classifier and severity head
Held-out evaluation3248,000every figure in this brief

Held-out shapes come from a separate RNG stream over deliberately wider ranges than training — ceilings 22,000–32,000 ft against 24,000–30,000 ft — so the test set measures generalisation, not memory. A nearest-neighbour search in standardised telemetry space confirms no held-out frame is a near-duplicate of a training frame: closest approach 0.126 σ, median 0.340 σ.

Boundaries

What this is not

A reviewer who finds an unstated limitation discounts everything else. So these are stated rather than left to be discovered.

Everything is simulated The engine, the instrumentation and the faults are models. No hardware-in-the-loop validation has been performed, and nothing here is certified for airworthiness.
The estimator is not an EKF It is a linear Kalman filter with a complementary prediction step toward the physics model. No non-linear transition function, no Jacobian — so the covariance is a tuned noise-rejection parameter, not a calibrated uncertainty.
The attribution is not SHAP It is gradient × input: a first-order local ranking chosen for the 50 ms edge budget, without Shapley local-accuracy or consistency guarantees.
The RUL interval is a trend interval Built from the spread of the degradation fit, not from a distribution-free coverage guarantee. It should not be described as a calibrated 95 % interval.
Per-frame inference degrades under extreme noise At five times instrumentation sigma the classifier names a false mechanical fault on about one frame in six. The annunciator votes over a rolling window, so it does not reach the operator — but it is a real weakness.
The MVEM fit figure is near-circular The model interpolates the same derating table it is then scored against. Treat the 0.00 HP RMSE as a consistency check, not as independent validation.
From demonstrator to flyable

What each next step actually requires

now → +3 months

Bench validation

Swap the simulated sensor layer for a CAN/RS-485 shim behind the same interface and run against a test-cell engine. Nothing downstream changes. Delivers the measured MVEM fit error we do not yet have.

+3 → +6 months

Recalibration on real data

Refit the MVEM constants and per-channel sigma to the bench engine, retrain the autoencoder on the resulting healthy residuals, re-run the full suite. Expect the false-alarm rate to move most.

+6 → +9 months

Edge port

Target a Jetson Orin Nano or Cortex-A class flight computer. Export both networks to ONNX, profile on target, confirm behaviour at operating temperature. Replaces the estimate with a measurement.

+9 → +18 months

Flight-representative integration

Signed frames and encrypted downlink, redundant recording, and a DO-178C-style requirements trace. The deterministic parts trace conventionally; the learned components need a partitioned-assurance argument.
